# Stayful Qualification Framework

**Training confidence: strong** — 121 transcripts analysed (110 pre-existing + 1 this run: entry 102, Karen Lee), against thresholds low <15 / building 15–30 / good 30–50 / strong 50+. Per-profile-type confidence varies: STL-SW (45 entries) is **strong**; EX-STL/PURCH/ABROAD (19–22 entries each) are **good–strong**; SELL (8) and R2R (6) remain **building**.

## What to Establish Before Showing Figures

In roughly this order, before the income walkthrough begins:

1. **Situation** — do they already own it (STL-SW/EX-STL), are they buying it (PURCH), selling it (SELL), relocating (ABROAD), or arranging a rent-to-rent deal (R2R)? Note: leads who inherited a property via probate, or who are converting a never-let/new-build property, currently get filed under PURCH or STL-SW as the nearest fit — the taxonomy has a known gap here (flagged again this run, entry 102 — a never-let new-build conversion mid-completion) and a dedicated INHERIT/NEW-BUILD code is overdue.
2. **Tenancy end date** — is there a sitting tenant (including a family member as tenant), and is notice formally served or only discussed verbally?
3. **Purchase stage** — offer accepted, exchanged, or completed? Off-plan build date if relevant? Or, for an inherited property, is probate complete and is legal possession confirmed? For a new-build/conversion, is the build itself actually finished (not just "nearly") — entry 102 showed this can gate the whole furnish-for-STL decision even before a normal qualification conversation starts.
4. **Moving confirmation** — is the departure date firm (employer/visa/sale-confirmed) or still soft? Note this also applies to purely domestic relocations with a long runway, and to a "possible plan" abroad with no firm date at all — treat the same as DLY-MOV even without a confirmed date.
5. **Furnishing status** — fully furnished, partially, or empty? Self-furnish or Stayful-coordinated? For a property not yet furnished at all (pre-decision, as in entry 102), be candid and specific in furnishing recommendations (bed configuration, workspace, minimal furniture for lower cleaning/breakage risk) even before the commercial decision is made — this builds trust ahead of the sale, not after it.
6. **Mortgage/ownership type** — residential, BTL, no mortgage (owned outright/inherited), or a financing vehicle (SSAS, off-plan, or an SPV/limited-company restructure being considered) that may restrict STL use. No-mortgage leads should be qualified specifically for downside-protection framing (see meeting-runbook.md Phase 3), not upside-maximisation framing. **Never give live mortgage-type advice from memory** — a standard buy-to-let switch was told to a lead as sufficient in a prior run when a holiday-let product or written lender consent was actually required; say you'll confirm and follow up in writing instead.
7. **Building/lease eligibility for any flat, leasehold, or managed-building property** — has the freeholder or building management ever confirmed short-letting is permitted? Ask directly and get it in writing before proceeding to fees or a go-live date. Do not treat an already-live Airbnb listing (EX-STL profile) as proof — two prior deals reached agreed terms, one with a signed offer, before this surfaced as a hard "no" and killed both.
8. **Motivation** — escaping a specific pain (rate shock, bad tenant, empty property, chronic student/HMO maintenance issues) vs. maximising a new asset vs. bridging a sale vs. preserving a family asset long-term.
9. **Timeline** — what specific external event, if any, gates when they could actually go live? For a mid-build/mid-conversion property, this may be a construction milestone rather than a person or process — ask for the realistic completion window directly (entry 102: "kitchen's going in, grass is being laid... still a few weeks").
10. **Prior knowledge** — have they already spoken to another agent, a developer's in-house management, or researched figures themselves (including comparing Stayful's estimate against other sites' differing numbers)?
11. **Competitor contact** — is a competing management company, guaranteed-rent scheme, or developer offer already live in parallel? Also check whether the lead is already self/friend-managing an informal Airbnb — this is a strong green light (GL-SELF), not a competitor risk, and should shortcut straight to commission/scope-of-service questions rather than re-pitching the STL concept — but see point 7, this does not itself confirm building eligibility.

## Question Sequence (from response-library/questions.json — not re-audited this run, see manifest data_gaps for known size/coverage caveats)

Highest-frequency questions actually asked, in roughly the order most meetings naturally raise them:

1. **Contract terms** — "Is there a minimum tie-in period? What's the exit process? Is there an early-exit fee?"
2. **Management fee** — "What's the fee and what's included? Does that cover cleaning too, or is that separate?" (Increasingly also: "Is that percentage VAT-inclusive, and is it factored into your net figure?" — and, in its plainest form, "if the guest is paying the cleaning, why are we paying the cleaning as well?", entry 102 — answer: it's a pass-through, the guest-paid figure is gross revenue before any deduction.)
3. **Guest type / who's actually staying** and **realistic income expectation** — these two run roughly concurrently once figures are on screen.
4. **Which platforms is it listed on**.
5. **STL vs LTL (or current HMO/student-let) financial comparison**.
6. **Council tax treatment when switching**, **guest vetting/property protection**, **can the property stay on the market while STL'd** (SELL-profile specific), **does STL income cover mortgage and running costs**, **do you offer any kind of income guarantee**, and **does the building/lease actually allow short-letting, and has that ever been formally confirmed**.

Note: meeting-phase tagging in the source data remains thin and inconsistent — treat the ordering above as frequency-derived, not a strictly validated sequence.

### By profile type — top questions (from by-profile-type files' `top_questions`)

- **STL-SW** (45 entries): "How does STL income compare to what I'm getting from a long-term let?" (freq 21/45 — dominant), then furnishing cost (8), fees (6), guest vetting/damage risk (6), council tax (4), mortgage compliance (4), income-guarantee/confidence-level asks (recurring theme). Entry 102 added: pricing-adjustment mechanics ("how do you adjust price up and down?") and whole-house vs per-room logic for a multi-bed HMO being considered as a second property.
- **EX-STL** (19 entries): "How does Stayful's fee/service compare to my current management company?" (4), "What income can I realistically expect including the worst-case month?" (5), "Does the building/lease actually permit short-letting, and has this ever been formally confirmed?" (the question that would have caught 092 Caixia Ye before it was lost).
- **PURCH** (21 entries): "What income can I realistically expect, and how does it compare to LTL?" (8), full cost/fee breakdown (5), strategy validation — platform vs direct bookings (3), furnishing/setup cost (4), and "Am I tied into a fixed contract term, and is there a route out if my circumstances change?" — specific to a downside-protection-seeking, no-mortgage lead.
- **SELL** (8 entries): "Can the property stay actively listed for sale while it's short-let?" (4 — the dominant SELL-specific question), "Will STL income cover the mortgage and running costs?" (3).
- **ABROAD** (22 entries): "How will the property actually be managed with no one on-site?" (7), income/LTL comparison (11 combined across two related question clusters), guest damage/vetting (6), "Is short-letting even permitted here — leasehold/freeholder consent, or does my mortgage allow it?" (4), and "Which insurance takes precedence if something goes wrong — mine or yours?" (2).
- **R2R** (6 entries): "Does this deal work financially once rent, fee, and setup costs are all stacked?" (4 — dominant; almost never resolves in the lead's favour at the landlord's initial asking rent).

## Delay Detection & Timeline-Probing Questions

Ask the specific timeline question tied to whichever delay type the trigger phrases suggest (from `by-delay-type/*.json`):

| Delay type | Entries | Timeline question to ask |
|---|---|---|
| **DLY-TEN** (existing tenant, incl. family member) | 11 | "Once your tenant/occupant is actually out, is there a firm date for that yet, or is it still an estimate — and is notice formally served, or just discussed verbally?" |
| **DLY-FUR** (unfurnished) | 13 | "If you can send me the room dimensions and a couple of photos, I can turn that ballpark into a firm itemised quote — how soon could you get those over?" |
| **DLY-PUR** (purchasing, or no target property identified yet) | 12 | "Where are you in the process right now — has anything exchanged, and what's the realistic completion date your solicitor or the vendor has given you?" |
| **DLY-MOV** (moving/abroad, firm or soft plan, or a long-runway domestic relocation) | 12 | "Once you've got your move/departure date confirmed, roughly how much notice would you want to give us to get everything onboarded and live before you go?" |
| **DLY-OTH** (renovation/new-build completion, mortgage, third-party consent, spouse, compliance, probate, building/lease eligibility) | 35 | "Of everything still to sort before this could go live — [name the specific blocker back to them] — what's the realistic next milestone, and when do you expect to know more?" For building/lease eligibility specifically: "Has your freeholder or building management ever confirmed in writing that short-letting is allowed?" For a mid-build/conversion property (entry 102): ask directly whether the build itself is finished, since the whole meeting may be pre-decision groundwork rather than a live-property comparison. |

Typical resolution windows: DLY-FUR and DLY-OTH often resolve within weeks to ~3 months once a firm quote or milestone lands (except the building-eligibility subset of DLY-OTH, which is a hard stop, not a timing delay); DLY-TEN and DLY-MOV more often run 2–3 months (up to 6–12 in HMO, inherited-property, or soft-plan/long-notice cases); DLY-PUR typically 1–3 months. **Conversion-after-delay rates recorded: DLY-OTH 26%, DLY-FUR 31%, DLY-TEN 9%, DLY-MOV 8%, DLY-PUR 8%** — delays gated by a third party's process outside anyone's control (DLY-PUR, DLY-TEN, DLY-MOV, and externally-gated DLY-OTH cases like probate or building eligibility) convert markedly worse than delays the lead can resolve themselves (DLY-FUR, most of DLY-OTH).

## Green Light Confirmation Questions

The dominant green-light signal across nearly every archetype and profile type is **GL-MGMT** — a stated preference for fully hands-off management. Confirm it directly rather than assuming it: *"Just so I've got this right — you'd want this to be genuinely hands-off, where the only thing you're dealing with is the mortgage and bills?"*

Confirm **GL-TIME** by naming the actual external deadline back to them (a mortgage switch, a lease renewal, a relocation date) rather than accepting a vague "soon." Check whether the date confirmed is for the letting itself or for a separate blocker (e.g. a mortgage restructuring) that may have no firm date of its own.

Watch for **GL-SELF** — the lead proactively raising signing before the next call, asking about a multi-property discount, or volunteering a second property, or already self-managing a live, booked listing. This is the strongest unprompted signal and should shift the meeting toward Phase 7 (Close) in the runbook — but always still confirm building/lease eligibility (point 7 above) even here; GL-SELF from an already-operating listing is not proof of permission (092). This run's dataset's clearest positive example remains a lead already self/friend-managing a trading Airbnb (proving the concept before the call) who combined GL-SELF with GL-TIME (explicit urgency) and converted within an 8-minute call with an accepted bundle-discount offer (entry 086).

## Red Flags (from lost-reason-patterns.json — 15 total lost entries)

- **Building/lease does not permit short-letting, discovered only after commercial terms or an offer were agreed** — top-line reason (2 of 15 lost entries, tied for the largest single cause), both leasehold/flat properties. Screen for this explicitly during qualification (point 7 above), before quoting fees or a go-live date, not after.
- **R2R deals where landlord rent leaves no real margin after Stayful's fee and setup cost** — 3 of 4 lost R2R leads failed specifically on this gate; pre-qualify rent level vs. STL gross-revenue ceiling *before* booking the web meeting.
- **Very low existing mortgage (e.g. ~£260/month)** — limited financial pain means the STL uplift rarely clears the setup-cost bar; flag early and consider a capital-compounding reframe rather than a cost-reduction pitch. (Contrast with the *zero*-mortgage/owned-outright case, which instead responds well to downside-protection framing — see meeting-runbook.md. Entry 102, also zero-mortgage/owned outright, responded well to this framing too: "all net is cash flow".)
- **Property purchase still mid-process** — 3 of 21 PURCH leads were lost specifically while the purchase itself was still live (chose a developer's in-house management, or lost momentum before completion); this is the highest-risk window for PURCH leads specifically.
- **Shared-accommodation/HMO-style property** — outside Stayful's operating model entirely; 1 lost R2R lead was disqualified on this basis alone, independent of financial viability. Screen for this on the qualifying call, before booking a full web meeting. Note: a *whole-house* HMO listing (not per-room) is a different, viable case — see entry 102's Northampton 6-bed HMO discussion, recommended as whole-house.
- **Trust gap from a prior management company's broken promises**, left unresolved by pitch alone rather than verified proof — flagged as a lost reason for an EX-STL-type lead.
- **Competitor chosen or deferred** — most likely when the lead is evaluating multiple providers simultaneously (2–3 agents) and no differentiated proof point (case study, specific local track record) was offered.

## Green Flags (from offer-timing-intelligence.json — 46 offers analysed)

- The strongest recorded green-light combination remains **GL-MGMT + GL-SELF + GL-TIME together** (seen in 4 offers pre-086) — none of those were explicitly recorded as accepted live on the call, but the dataset's confirmed acceptance (entry 086, GL-SELF + GL-TIME, no GL-MGMT explicitly stated) shows readiness signals can close a deal live even without all three; a future pass should re-run this combination analysis including it.
- **Offer rate by profile type** (stale as of 2026-08-30, not recomputed this run): SELL leads receive an offer 87.5% of the time (highest), EX-STL 70.6%, STL-SW 40.5%, PURCH 27.8%, ABROAD 29.4% — reflecting how much more often SELL and EX-STL leads present with a clear, resolvable financial case by the close of the meeting. EX-STL's acceptance rate is 8.3%, the best of any profile type with offer data.
- **Best offer timing across every profile type is "closing"** — no profile type in the dataset shows an offer landing effectively earlier in the meeting, with one exception: a proactive bundle-discount offer made the instant a multi-property signal appears, which does not need to wait for the formal close.
- **Caution (carried forward):** a green light and an offer can both be present and the deal can still be lost later if building/lease eligibility was never confirmed — treat eligibility as a gate that sits alongside, not beneath, the green-light checklist.
- **No offer was made in entry 102** — correctly: the property is still mid-build and the decision is joint with an absent spouse. A forced deadline here would have pressured a lead who cannot yet honour it; the milestone-based follow-up approach (see DLY-OTH handling) was used instead.

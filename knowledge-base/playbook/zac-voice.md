# Zac's Voice

**Training confidence: strong** — synthesized from 109 transcripts (`do-not-say.json`: 48 flagged moments; `closing-scripts.json`: 31 closes analysed; 8 archetype and 6 profile-type files). Archetype-level voice data is uneven — see "By Archetype" below for which archetypes have zero logged entries.

---

## Overall Tone

Zac's delivery across transcripts is direct, unhurried, and willing to concede a point when the honest answer doesn't favour Stayful — he tells leads self-furnishing might be cheaper, that a competing advisor "is right," or flatly that a deal's margin "doesn't work," rather than talking past it. He reaches for concrete arithmetic over reassurance: worst-case months, specific £ deductions, and named comparable properties, done live on the call rather than asserted. He rarely oversells — "I'm not going to sit here and say it's not going to be positive, because it will be" is characteristic: confident, but stopping short of a guarantee. The tone is collaborative rather than pitchy — closes read more like "here's what I'll do next" than a hard ask. New this run: when a lead directly pressed for a confidence level rather than accepting a projection, Zac's answer stayed candid rather than inflating — "maybe not loads more than the HMO" — and pivoted to real, non-income differentiators (control, hassle, competition, spec) instead of oversell.

## How He Opens, Transitions, Handles Numbers, Reassures, Emphasises

**Numbers, delivered live and broken down, not just stated:**
- *"The only thing you'll have to deal with is paying the mortgage and the bills, essentially. And we'll deal with everything else."*
- *"I don't see any way, if I'm being honest, looking at these figures... that you won't be profitable"*
- *"Even if I'm 20% wrong, we're still profitable"*
- *"Even if I'm 50% off, you're still doing a thousand pounds of profit. I'd have to have done something seriously wrong in order to be 50% off"* — the sharper version of the same pattern, used with a no-mortgage, downside-protection-seeking lead
- *"We normally say this is within a sort of 10% accuracy range"*
- *"I don't see a situation where you're going to be struggling to find the money for the mortgage"*
- *"The worst case isn't really likely to affect your current situation because it's essentially your lowest case is what you were already making"* — anchoring the floor directly against a lead's own known current HMO/student-let income

**Reassurance, mechanics-first rather than sentiment-first:**
- *"You won't ever need to go to the property itself once we've got everything set up"*
- *"The Slack channel becomes your eyes and ears on the property"*
- *"It's not any surprises when you get the statement at the end of the month"*
- *"You won't need to be at the property or near the property to come on board — we manage for people that were in other countries"*

**Reframing a weak-looking number honestly rather than dressing it up:**
- *"Any amount you're really making is a cost-saving exercise for you as well as a profit-making one"*
- *"It's about reducing outgoings, not maximising profit, while the property is on the market"* (SELL leads)
- *"It would most likely be more expensive if we furnish it, because there's labour included on our side"* — conceding a point against Stayful's own setup-quote sale
- *"Even if you fully furnished it... you should get more on the long let anyway, because you're now renting a furnished property"* — reframing a setup spend as an asset-value investment, not just an STL cost

**Transitioning into the close (from `closing-scripts.json`):**
- *"All right, well, what I'll do then after this call is I'll just send a bit of a summary of everything we've spoken about, I'll also put in like a bit of an action plan so we've got some estimated time frames as well."*
- *"Is there anything you want to go through specifically about the income... any specific questions you got about the service, or I can just go through it."*
- *"Based off of what we're, what we've discussed, does it look like it'd be of interest to you, or does it look like it's something that wouldn't work for you?"*
- *"I mean if we're doing both of them I'm happy to do a discount as well on the management for you if that works"* — the proactive bundle-discount offer, made unprompted, that produced the dataset's first confirmed on-call acceptance

**Diagnosing underperformance for EX-STL leads without blaming the property:**
- *"This isn't a property issue you're having. It's a listing-establishment issue and it's a management-company issue."*
- *"We take on all the changeovers — you're never the manual cleaner"*
- *"Every review attached to your specific property, not a shared hotel-style listing"*

## Objection-Handling Pattern

The consistent shape across `objections.json` and the archetype files: **name the mechanism, not the reassurance.** Instead of "don't worry, guests are vetted," the pattern that actually resolves objections is a specific deposit figure, ID-check process, and insurance ceiling. Instead of disputing a lead's own lower revenue estimate, Zac reconciles it with a specific evidenced explanation (a review gap, a price premium) rather than a defensive dismissal. Where he genuinely doesn't know — cross-border tax, a specific planning question — he says so plainly and offers to find out, rather than guessing. Where a lead's own real operational experience conflicts with Stayful's stated preference (this run: a lead's smart locks "never failed" versus Zac's key-safe preference), the pattern is to acknowledge the lead's evidence explicitly rather than override it with policy alone — left as an open, respectful disagreement rather than a false resolution.

## By Archetype

- **certainty-seeker** (11 entries): establish process rigor and credibility early, before the headline figure — this archetype tests the honesty of the numbers before trusting them.
- **loss-averse-switcher** (6 entries): get the co-decision-maker on the call from the outset — an absent partner is the single most common blocker, not the offer itself.
- **analytical-evaluator** (17 entries): open with the methodology behind the figures before the number itself — this archetype trusts process over promises.
- **fast-path-gain-focused** (2 entries, both converted with an accepted offer): open efficiently, lead with revenue benchmarked against current position, and be ready to apply a discount live at the meeting.
- **urgency-driven, status-quo-resistant, betrayal-damaged, social-proof-dependent**: **zero logged entries in the current dataset.** No meeting guidance can be honestly synthesized for these four archetypes yet — do not improvise a voice pattern for them from general sales instinct.

## Never Say (top patterns, from do-not-say.json's 48 flagged moments, grouped by underlying cause and ranked by frequency)

1. **Leaving a live concern only partially addressed** (~14 of 48 flagged moments — the single largest category). E.g. a lead presses "is that changing the figures negatively?" and gets a partial answer that isn't followed up in writing. *Fix: close the loop in a same-day written follow-up, don't let it ride on the call alone.*
2. **A cost or fee point left as a range instead of a firm figure at the moment it matters** (7). Furnishing ballparks, exit-fee scenarios pressed three times, an unresolved bifold-door cost — each left live doubt hanging into the close. *Fix: convert to a firm, itemised number the same day.*
3. **A technical/audio/connectivity failure derailing the flow** (7) — screen share failing for an entire call, audio dropping at the open, a lead unable to read the shared screen. *Fix: have a fallback (read figures aloud, send the deck immediately) ready before it happens.*
4. **Inconsistent or misspoken figures** (6) — a worst-month figure stated as £600 then corrected to £2,100; the analyser misreading bedroom count live. *Fix: never introduce a number you haven't just double-checked out loud.*
5. **Getting the lead's name or identity wrong** (3) — addressing a lead by the wrong name, confusing two leads' bookings. *Fix: confirm identity against the Monday record before the call starts, not during it.*
6. **A flat, under-informed answer to a direct ask** (3) — leading with "we don't offer guaranteed returns" before any supporting data; confirming a funding vehicle (SSAS) simply won't work with no immediate alternative offered. *Fix: pair any hard "no" with the next concrete option in the same breath.*
7. **An unclear or tightly-framed deadline** (2) — a lead misreading a month-long offer window as three days; a lead reacting "okay, so that's a bit of pressure" to a one-month expiry. *Fix: state the exact date twice and confirm the lead has heard it correctly.*
8. **An automated tool contradicting what's said live** (3, new example this run) — a website assessment tool telling a lead the property was "not suitable"; a generated presentation containing a materially wrong comparison figure; a cost-hierarchy slide silently omitting cleaning/laundry, understating true costs by ~£18,000 against the correct forecast. *Fix: cross-check auto-generated output against the actual property and the source-of-truth forecast before the call, and fix the generator itself once a defect is found rather than only patching it verbally on the call.*
9. **Being unable to back a claim with checkable evidence, or dismissing a lead's own real evidence** (3, new example this run) — unable to name or locate Stayful's own managed properties in a specific area when asked directly; declining a lead's repeated request for a reference call or property visit without offering an alternative; stating a preference (key safes) without first acknowledging a lead's own contradicting track record (smart locks that have never failed). *Fix: keep a locality-specific reference sheet ready before every call, always have an alternative on hand, and acknowledge real evidence before restating a differing preference.*
